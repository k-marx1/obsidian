### Task 1. Research the Terms

1. **Mineral Resources**  
    Natural deposits of minerals found in the Earth’s crust that are exploited for use in various industries such as construction, manufacturing, and energy production.
    
2. **Ferrous Metals**  
    Metals that contain iron (commonly referred to as “black metals”), such as steel and cast iron. They are typically magnetic and valued for their strength and durability.
    
3. **Non-Ferrous Metals**  
    Metals that either do not contain iron or contain it only in trace amounts. These metals include copper, aluminum, zinc, lead, tin, magnesium, and titanium. They are often prized for their light weight, high electrical conductivity, corrosion resistance, and non-magnetic properties.
    
4. **Conductivity**  
    The ability of a material to conduct electric current. For example, copper is renowned for its high specific electrical conductivity, making it essential in electrical wiring and electronics.
    
5. **Alloy**  
    A homogeneous mixture composed of two or more elements, at least one of which is a metal. Alloys are designed to enhance desirable properties such as strength, corrosion resistance, and conductivity. Common examples include brass (a mixture of copper and zinc) and bronze (a mixture of copper and tin).

### Task 5. Discussion Questions

16. **What kinds of mineral resources do you know?**  
    Mineral resources can be broadly categorized into metallic and non-metallic resources. Metallic resources are further divided into ferrous (black metals) and non-ferrous (colored metals). Additionally, energy resources like oil, gas, and coal are also considered mineral resources in a broader sense.
    
17. **What is the difference between ferrous and non-ferrous metals?**  
    Ferrous metals contain iron and usually exhibit magnetic properties, making them strong and durable. In contrast, non-ferrous metals do not contain iron; they are typically lighter, offer higher electrical conductivity, and have better resistance to corrosion and non-magnetic properties.
    
18. **Can you name 7 non-ferrous metals?**  
    Yes. Examples include:
    
    - Copper
    - Aluminum
    - Zinc
    - Lead
    - Tin
    - Magnesium
    - Titanium
19. **What applications are non-ferrous metals used for?**  
    Non-ferrous metals are widely used in applications where light weight, high conductivity, and corrosion resistance are required. For instance, copper is used in electrical wiring; aluminum in aerospace and automotive industries for lightweight construction; zinc for galvanization to prevent corrosion; and other non-ferrous metals are utilized in electronics, construction, and various specialized components.
    
20. **What are the distinctive properties of non-ferrous metals?**  
    The distinctive properties include:
    
    - Lower density (lightweight)
    - High electrical conductivity (as seen in copper)
    - Excellent resistance to corrosion
    - Non-magnetic behavior  
        These properties make them ideal for a range of industrial and technological applications.
21. **What is an alloy?**  
    An alloy is a homogeneous mixture of two or more elements, at least one of which is a metal. Alloys are created to enhance certain properties, such as strength, durability, corrosion resistance, and electrical conductivity. Classic examples include brass (an alloy of copper and zinc) and bronze (an alloy of copper and tin).
